Your selling personality

Your happy place is the studio, and selling feels like a distraction from making. You're thoughtful, often introverted, and your work is personal and one of a kind. Talking about prices makes you squirm. You'd love to be taken seriously as an artist and have a professional champion your work.

Why it suits you

A gallery handles the display, marketing, collectors and the sales conversation, usually for a 30–50% commission. In return you get credibility, higher prices and more time in the studio.

Watch out for

Galleries look for a consistent, recognisable body of work, and you'll still need to show up at openings now and then. They rarely take complete beginners, so juried shows, open calls and artist-run spaces are your stepping stones. Price so that both you and the gallery do well from the commission.

Your first three steps

1Build a cohesive series of 10–15 pieces that clearly belong together.
2Enter local juried shows and open calls to start your artist CV.
3Research three galleries that show work like yours. Go to their openings and follow their submission guidelines exactly.

Your free checklist

The Studio Devotee's Getting-Started Checklist

Choose your focus: the subject, medium or style you want to be known for
Create a cohesive series of 10–15 pieces and photograph each one square on, in even light
Write a short artist statement (about 150 words), a bio and a simple artist CV
Keep an inventory: title, size, medium, date, price and where each piece is
Know your Minimum Price and Ideal Price, and check they still work after a 40–50% commission
Go to openings and shortlist two or three true galleries that show work like yours
Read each gallery's submission guidelines and follow them exactly
Before you sign, check exclusivity, how long work stays, rotation and who pays for what
Check your insurance covers your work in transit and on consignment
Keep building your own email list: the gallery's customer list isn't yours
Check your business licence and tax registration, and add a copyright line to every invoice
Follow up politely, and keep making while you wait
